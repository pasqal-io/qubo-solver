## Local-Energy-Scale Drive Shaper

`LocalEnergyScaleDriveShaper` constructs a fixed analog drive from the local
physical energy scale of each qubit. It uses both:

- the final detuning derived from the QUBO diagonal;
- the physical interactions generated by the embedded register.

No classical pulse-optimization loop is performed.

The shaper returns:

- a generated `Drive`, ready to be compiled and simulated;
- an empty `Solution` placeholder.

The measured bitstrings, probabilities, and costs are produced later, when the
quantum sequence is executed.

---

### Principle

Let \(Q\) be the QUBO matrix and let \(N\) be the number of variables.

The final target detuning associated with qubit \(i\) is derived from the QUBO
diagonal:

$$
d_i = -\frac{Q_{ii}}{2}.
$$

For each pair of atoms \(i\) and \(j\), the embedded register generates a
physical interaction

$$
V_{ij} = \frac{1}{r_{ij}^{6}},
$$

where \(r_{ij}\) is the distance between the two atoms in the normalized
register.

The local physical energy scale of qubit \(i\) is defined as

$$
E_i =
\left|\delta_i(T)\right|
+
\sum_{j\neq i}\left|V_{ij}\right|,
$$

where \(\delta_i(T)\) is the final detuning effectively assigned to qubit
\(i\).

The average local energy scale is

$$
\overline{E}
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N} E_i.
$$

The peak Rabi frequency is then chosen as

$$
\Omega_{\max}
=
\kappa \overline{E},
$$

where \(\kappa > 0\) is the user-defined
`local_energy_scale_kappa`.

This rule makes the amplitude depend on both the local detuning scale and the
interaction energy of the embedded register.

---

### Final Detuning Encoding

#### With DMM enabled

When a Detuning Map Modulator (DMM) is available, the final target detunings are
encoded through a global detuning and site-dependent DMM weights.

Let

$$
d_{\min} = \min_i d_i,
\qquad
d_{\max} = \max_i d_i,
$$

and define the detuning spread

$$
\Delta d = d_{\max} - d_{\min}.
$$

If \(\Delta d > 0\), the final detuning components are

$$
\delta_g(T) = d_{\max},
$$

$$
\delta_{\mathrm{dmm}}(T)
=
-\Delta d
=
-\left(d_{\max}-d_{\min}\right),
$$

and

$$
w_i
=
\frac{d_{\max}-d_i}
     {d_{\max}-d_{\min}}
\in [0,1].
$$

The final local detuning is therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta_i(T)
&=
\delta_g(T)
+
\delta_{\mathrm{dmm}}(T)w_i \\
&=
d_{\max}
-
\left(d_{\max}-d_{\min}\right)
\frac{d_{\max}-d_i}
     {d_{\max}-d_{\min}} \\
&=
d_i.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, before hardware rescaling, the DMM encoding reproduces every target
detuning exactly.

The negative sign of \(\delta_{\mathrm{dmm}}(T)\) is required because weighted
DMM waveforms must be non-positive.

If the detuning spread is numerically negligible, the DMM is not used and the
method falls back to a global final detuning.

#### Without DMM

If `dmm=False`, or if the selected device does not support DMM, all atoms
receive the same final global detuning:

$$
\delta_i(T)
=
\delta_g(T)
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N} d_i.
$$

No weighted detuning map is created.

---

### Local Energy Scale With DMM

When DMM is active, the local energy scale is computed from the encoded local
detunings:

$$
E_i
=
|d_i|
+
\sum_{j\neq i}|V_{ij}|.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\Omega_{\max}
=
\kappa
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left(
|d_i|
+
\sum_{j\neq i}|V_{ij}|
\right).
$$

---

### Local Energy Scale Without DMM

Without DMM, every atom receives the mean target detuning

$$
\overline{d}
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_i.
$$

The local energy scale becomes

$$
E_i
=
|\overline{d}|
+
\sum_{j\neq i}|V_{ij}|,
$$

and the peak amplitude is

$$
\Omega_{\max}
=
\kappa
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left(
|\overline{d}|
+
\sum_{j\neq i}|V_{ij}|
\right).
$$

---

### Schedule Shape

The method uses the maximum sequence duration declared by the device. If the
device does not specify one, it uses a fallback duration of \(1000\).

#### Amplitude

The amplitude follows a flat-top interpolated profile:

$$
\Omega(t)
=
\left[
\varepsilon,
\Omega_{\max},
\Omega_{\max},
\varepsilon
\right],
$$

where

$$
\varepsilon = 10^{-9}.
$$

A small positive value is used at both endpoints instead of an exact zero.

#### Global detuning

The initial detuning is

$$
\delta_0
=
-\max_i |d_i|.
$$

The global detuning waveform is

$$
\delta_g(t)
=
\left[
\delta_0,
\delta_0,
\delta_g(T),
\delta_g(T)
\right].
$$

The sequence therefore starts at a negative detuning and ends at the global
target detuning.

#### DMM weighted detuning

When DMM is active, the method creates a weighted detuning waveform using:

- the site-dependent weights \(w_i\);
- the final DMM detuning \(\delta_{\mathrm{dmm}}(T)\);
- the full sequence duration.

The DMM contribution is generated with `constant_weighted_dmm(...)`.

---

### Hardware Bounds

The raw amplitude is

$$
\Omega_{\max}^{(\mathrm{raw})}
=
\kappa\overline{E}.
$$

Let \(\Omega_{\mathrm{device}}\) denote the maximum amplitude compilable for
the selected device and register.

The applied amplitude is

$$
\Omega_{\max}
=
\min
\left(
\Omega_{\max}^{(\mathrm{raw})},
\Omega_{\mathrm{device}}
\right).
$$

A warning is emitted when amplitude clamping is required.

The maximum detuning compatible with the selected amplitude is computed as

$$
\delta_{\max}^{(\mathrm{allowed})}
=
\rho_{\delta/\Omega}
\Omega_{\max}
\left(1-10^{-3}\right),
$$

where \(\rho_{\delta/\Omega}\) is the device detuning-to-amplitude ratio.

If

$$
\max_i |d_i|
>
\delta_{\max}^{(\mathrm{allowed})},
$$

all target detunings are uniformly rescaled:

$$
d_i
\leftarrow
d_i
\frac{
\delta_{\max}^{(\mathrm{allowed})}
}{
\max_j |d_j|
}.
$$

After rescaling, the global detuning, DMM amplitude, and DMM weights are
recomputed.

The uniform scaling preserves the relative structure of the target detunings.

---

### Register Requirements

The interaction contribution is computed from the embedded register:

$$
V_{ij} = \frac{1}{r_{ij}^{6}}.
$$

Therefore, the generated drive depends on the physical distances produced by
the embedding method.

The register must:

- contain exactly one atom for each QUBO variable;
- use qubit identifiers consistent with the QUBO variable ordering;
- satisfy the distance and geometry constraints of the selected device.

Register normalization can be controlled through `EmbeddingConfig`, including
the `min_distance` parameter.

---

### Configuration Parameters

| Field | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `drive_shaping_method` | `DriveType \| str` | Must be `DriveType.LOCAL_ENERGY_SCALE` or `"local_energy_scale"` |
| `dmm` | `bool` | Enables site-dependent final detunings when DMM is supported |
| `local_energy_scale_kappa` | `float` | Proportionality factor between the average local physical energy scale and \(\Omega_{\max}\); default: `0.25` |

These parameters are provided through `DriveShapingConfig`.

---

### Comparison With Other Drive Shapers

#### Proportional-Diagonal Drive Shaper

`ProportionalDiagonalDriveShaper` chooses the amplitude from the largest
absolute diagonal-derived detuning:

$$
\Omega_{\max}
=
\kappa
\max_i |d_i|.
$$

`LocalEnergyScaleDriveShaper` instead uses the average combined detuning and
interaction scale:

$$
\Omega_{\max}
=
\kappa
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left(
|\delta_i(T)|
+
\sum_{j\neq i}|V_{ij}|
\right).
$$

The local-energy-scale method therefore includes physical information from the
embedded register.

#### Bayesian-Search Drive Shaper

`BayesianSearchDriveShaper` optimizes pulse parameters through repeated
classical optimization and quantum simulation.

`LocalEnergyScaleDriveShaper` does not perform numerical pulse optimization.
It generates one drive directly from the QUBO diagonal, the embedded register,
the device limits, and \(\kappa\).

---

### Notes and Limitations

- The method uses the QUBO diagonal to define the target final detunings.
- It uses the physical register interactions to determine the amplitude scale.
- The result depends on the embedding because the interaction terms depend on
  the inter-atomic distances.
- With DMM, variable target detunings can be encoded individually.
- Without DMM, the target detunings are replaced by their mean.
- Hardware constraints can clamp the amplitude or uniformly rescale the target
  detunings.
- The method is heuristic. It does not guarantee that the optimal QUBO
  bitstring will be sampled.
- The default value `local_energy_scale_kappa = 0.25` is a fallback value and
  can be adjusted for benchmarking or device calibration.

---

### Example

```python
from qubosolver import (
    DriveShapingConfig,
    DriveType,
    Instance,
    Solver,
    SolverConfig,
    matrix,
)

qubo = matrix.tensor(
    [
        [-6.0, 2.0, 2.0, 2.0],
        [2.0, -7.5, 2.0, 2.0],
        [2.0, 2.0, -7.5, 2.0],
        [2.0, 2.0, 2.0, -7.0],
    ]
)

instance = Instance(
    matrix=qubo,
)

config = SolverConfig(
    use_quantum=True,
    drive_shaping=DriveShapingConfig(
        drive_shaping_method=(
            DriveType.LOCAL_ENERGY_SCALE
        ),
        dmm=True,
        local_energy_scale_kappa=0.25,
    ),
)

solver = Solver(
    instance,
    config,
)

solution = solver.solve()

print(solution)
```

The same method can be selected with its string value:

```python
drive_shaping=DriveShapingConfig(
    drive_shaping_method="local_energy_scale",
    dmm=True,
    local_energy_scale_kappa=0.25,
)
```
